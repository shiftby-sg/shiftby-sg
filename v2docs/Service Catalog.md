ShiftBy helps organizations prepare knowledge for AI, keep AI-supported work aligned with its intended purpose, and sustain human responsibility throughout its use.

Our services draw on three complementary ShiftBy capabilities: **Organizational Memory, Bounded AI, and Human–AI Accountability**. We work through bounded advisory, assessment, architecture and control design, testing, and enablement engagements. Each engagement starts with a defined enterprise problem and concludes with evidence, findings, recommendations, and agreed next steps.

# Organizational Memory

**Preserve the context that makes organizational knowledge useful through change.**

Organizational Memory helps organizations preserve, connect, recover, and responsibly reuse knowledge, relationships, provenance, prior state, uncertainty, and learning. Knowledge graphs, retrieval systems, and memory mechanisms can support this contribution; their presence alone does not establish continuity of understanding.

## AI areas

- **Knowledge Graphs and Semantic Models:** concepts, relationships, provenance, and their representation.

- **AI Context and Memory:** retained context, historical state, freshness, and responsible reuse.

- **RAG and Enterprise Retrieval:** source readiness, retrieval design, and the quality of retrieved and generated results.

The first two areas are served through AI Knowledge and Context Architecture. Retrieval-focused needs are served through RAG and Enterprise Retrieval Assessment.

## Services

### AI Knowledge and Context Architecture

For organizations whose knowledge is fragmented, difficult to interpret, or unsuitable for reuse by people and AI.

Assess or design how a defined knowledge domain preserves meaning, relationships, source authority, provenance, and historical state. Scope can focus on semantic modelling, knowledge-graph architecture, or the lifecycle of retained AI context and memory. Clarify ownership, review, and acceptance of changes.

Knowledge-continuity work can examine context at risk during personnel, supplier, or system changes without requiring an AI system or commissioning architecture design.

**Typical outputs:** a knowledge/context assessment or architecture, relevant semantic and relationship models, lifecycle and ownership requirements, and improvement priorities.

### RAG and Enterprise Retrieval Assessment

For organizations preparing a retrieval use case or investigating unsuitable search and RAG results.

Select the scope appropriate to the system’s stage:

- **Readiness and architecture:** source suitability, permissions, data quality, content structure, metadata, freshness, and retrieval options.

- **Quality evaluation:** retrieval relevance and coverage, answer grounding, attribution, conflicting information, and unsupported-answer handling against agreed questions and criteria.

Readiness can be assessed before a system is runnable. Quality evaluation requires testable behaviour and suitable evaluation material; the two scopes need not be purchased together.

**Typical outputs:** readiness findings and architecture recommendations, or evaluation results and failure analysis, with prioritized improvements and verification criteria.

Retrieval quality and answer quality remain distinct. A relevant source or citation does not by itself establish a correct or suitable answer.

# Bounded AI

**Keep AI reasoning and execution aligned with the work intended.**

Bounded AI aims to reduce drift from agreed purpose, context, responsibilities, result requirements, and permitted progression. It applies to individual AI tasks, agents, and coordinated workflows through prepared inputs, explicit boundaries, validation, observability, and resource planning.

Here, drift concerns unintended changes in meaning, scope, or execution. It is distinct from statistical data or model drift. A sequence of predefined AI tasks does not need to be described as agentic.

## AI areas

- **AI Tasks and Reasoning:** prepared requests, context, responsibilities, result requirements, and validation.

- **AI Agents:** delegated discretion, tools, permitted actions, and intervention.

- **AI Workflows and Multi-Agent Systems:** coordination, handoffs, progression, exceptions, and cumulative resource limits.

These areas share one advisory service, scoped to the task, agent, or workflow that matters to the client. Measured quality testing can be commissioned through AI Evaluation and Reliability.

## Service

### AI Task, Agent and Workflow Advisory

For organizations that need an AI contribution to remain aligned with a defined business requirement.

Bound the engagement to one use case, responsibility, or workflow. Select the relevant work rather than automatically commissioning every activity:

- **Readiness and boundary assessment:** establish whether purpose, context, delegated discretion, permitted actions, and acceptance conditions are sufficiently defined; identify gaps.

- **Task and control design:** specify or review input/output requirements, reusable reasoning guidance, tool limits, approvals, validation, escalation, and stopping conditions.

- **Workflow review:** examine responsibilities, dependencies, handoffs, branches, correction paths, and the distinction between rule-governed progression and agent discretion.

- **Observability and resource planning:** review traceability of execution, validation outcomes, investigation needs, and time, token, and cost allowances across retries and delegated work.

**Typical outputs:** the relevant readiness findings, task or workflow specification, boundary/control model, observability and resource recommendations, and validation plan.

A design review assesses intended arrangements, not proof of operating effectiveness. Measured performance requires appropriate testing. Technical permissions do not establish organizational authority; ownership and intervention rights remain explicit.

Resource estimates remain separate from measured consumption and confirmed charges. Observability does not imply access to a model’s private internal reasoning.

# Human–AI Accountability

**Keep human ownership, authority, and answerability explicit throughout AI-enabled work.**

Human–AI Accountability applies across Organizational Memory and Bounded AI. It covers who owns knowledge and its acceptance, who authorizes AI participation, who reviews results and exceptions, and who can intervene or accept residual risk.

The following services can be commissioned directly or included within a knowledge, task, agent, or workflow engagement. Accountability is maintained throughout the work rather than added only at final approval.

## AI areas

- **AI Governance and Accountability:** ownership, decision rights, delegated authority, and risk acceptance.

- **Human Oversight:** practical review, challenge, intervention, and escalation.

- **AI Assurance and Evidence:** traceability and the evidence supporting reliance or review decisions.

Governance and oversight are addressed together through AI Governance and Human Oversight. Evidence-sufficiency questions are addressed through AI Assurance and Evidence Review.

## Services

### AI Governance and Human Oversight

For organizations with unclear responsibility for AI-enabled work or oversight that exists on paper but is difficult to exercise.

Assess or design accountability and oversight for a bounded use case. Cover ownership, legitimate authority, permitted use, supplier responsibilities, risk acceptance, review conditions, and escalation. Examine whether reviewers have the competence, information, time, authority, and practical ability to challenge, reject, pause, or override the work.

**Typical outputs:** an accountability and decision-rights map, governance or oversight findings, recommended operating arrangements, intervention requirements, and evidence expectations.

Oversight is proportionate to consequence and risk; not every output requires individual human approval. Relevant policies and frameworks can inform the work without constituting certification or a legal compliance opinion.

### AI Assurance and Evidence Review

For organizations deciding whether available evidence supports reliance on an AI-enabled activity, or preparing for internal or independent examination.

Review the connections among requirements, controls, tests, findings, exceptions, decisions, owners, and verification. Include decision/action traceability where material: purpose, evidence, rationale, authority, resulting action, observed state, and subsequent review.

Distinguish documented design from evidence of actual operation. Additional testing is separately scoped; this review does not assume controls are effective merely because they are documented.

**Typical outputs:** an evidence and traceability assessment, findings on the evidence supporting control effectiveness, unresolved limitations, and recommendations for the intended reliance or review decision.

A focused incident and learning review can also be commissioned separately. It examines selected incidents, exceptions, and remediation history to identify recurring weaknesses and corrective priorities, distinguishing contemporaneous records from later reconstruction. It is not a managed incident-response service.

# Services across the AI areas

Security and evaluation services apply across knowledge and retrieval systems, AI tasks, agents, and workflows. They can be commissioned separately or combined with the services above, according to the problem and agreed scope.

## AI areas

- **AI Security and Red Teaming:** security weaknesses, adversarial manipulation, and protection of trust boundaries.

- **AI Evaluation and Reliability:** measured quality, fitness for intended use, and verification after changes.

Each area has a corresponding service below. Both can apply across knowledge systems, tasks, agents, and workflows.

## Services

### AI Security and Red Teaming

For organizations that need to identify security weaknesses or examine behaviour under adversarial conditions.

Choose a security assessment, adversarial testing, or both:

- **Security assessment:** review the attack surface, trust boundaries, and controls of a defined AI application, RAG or memory system, agent, or workflow, with scoped testing where agreed.

- **Red teaming:** execute authorized adversarial scenarios against the agreed target. These may examine prompt injection, retrieval or memory manipulation, sensitive-information exposure, unsafe tool use, cross-agent trust, or approval bypass.

The method and target are explicit. A review does not imply attacks were executed, and a red-team exercise does not establish comprehensive security coverage. Testing remains within agreed authorization, targets, data handling, and operating constraints.

**Typical outputs:** security findings or adversarial test results, supporting evidence, remediation priorities, remaining uncertainties, and retest criteria.

### AI Evaluation and Reliability

For organizations seeking measured evidence that an AI task, agent, or workflow performs its intended responsibility.

Evaluate a defined component or end-to-end workflow against agreed criteria under normal, exceptional, and changing conditions. Relevant measures can include task performance, semantic fidelity, grounding, boundary adherence, progression, uncertainty handling, and regression after changes.

Retrieval-focused evaluations can be scoped through RAG and Enterprise Retrieval Assessment. The same testing work and findings are reused rather than sold as a duplicate assessment. Evaluation generates behavioural evidence; assurance review assesses whether the available evidence supports a reliance decision.

**Typical outputs:** an evaluation plan, acceptance criteria, observed results, failure analysis, and improvement or verification recommendations.

Reduced-drift claims require a stated baseline or justified comparator and comparable evidence. Completion, valid output structure, or the presence of controls alone does not demonstrate improvement.

**Remediation verification and retesting** can be commissioned as a follow-on or independently, including against findings from another provider where sufficient evidence is available. Scope can cover agreed quality, security, or control findings across retrieval, tasks, agents, and workflows. Outputs identify what was tested, which failure conditions remain, and the limitations of the result.

# How engagements work

An engagement can focus on one area or combine services around a single enterprise problem. Typical starting points include an underperforming RAG system, a new AI task, an agent receiving tool access, a multi-stage workflow losing meaning at handoffs, or unclear accountability for an AI-supported outcome. Related services reuse relevant evidence and findings; the agreed scope identifies any additional analysis, design, or testing required, avoiding duplicate assessments of the same material.

Before work begins, we agree:

- the business problem, accountable owner, and decisions the work should support;

- the use case, systems, information, and boundaries included;

- the evidence, access, and testing authorization required;

- the methods, deliverables, and acceptance criteria;

- known exclusions, dependencies, and limitations.

Workshops, coaching, training, and implementation guidance can support assessment, design, and adoption. Follow-on work may include refinement, client-team enablement, or retesting, according to the findings and agreed scope.

# Delivery boundaries

ShiftBy provides advisory, assessment, design, testing, and enablement within an agreed scope. Client teams or their delivery partners retain responsibility for production implementation, deployment, integration, and operation.

Clients retain organizational authority, consequential decisions, risk acceptance, and legal or regulatory responsibilities. Findings and recommendations inform those decisions; they do not transfer authority to ShiftBy or AI.

Service names describe engagement types, not fixed packages or guaranteed results. Scope, deliverables, timing, and fees are agreed for each engagement. Assessments do not guarantee error-free AI, zero drift, security, compliance, certification, or business outcomes.
