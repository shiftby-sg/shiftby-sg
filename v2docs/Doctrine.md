## 1. Founder proposition

> **Execution changes. Understanding preserves confidence.**

Organizations constantly change. Strategies evolve. Teams reorganize. Systems are replaced. Processes adapt. Technologies advance. AI accelerates analysis and execution.

Confidence does not come from preserving execution exactly as it was. Confidence comes from preserving the understanding required to explain, govern, and adapt execution as circumstances change.

ShiftBy exists to help organizations preserve, develop, and apply that understanding through change.

---

## 2. The organizational problem

Organizations often preserve artifacts more successfully than the understanding that gives those artifacts meaning.

Over time, decisions lose context, assumptions become invisible, evidence becomes difficult to recover, accountability becomes unclear, dependencies become hidden, knowledge is rediscovered, execution drifts from intent, and outcomes become disconnected from their origins.

This creates avoidable reconstruction effort, slower decisions, inconsistent interpretation, dependence on individual memory, and greater risk when work is transferred or automated.

ShiftBy addresses this problem by helping organizations preserve and apply understanding through change.

---

## 3. The business transformation

The business transformation is the organization’s improved ability to preserve and apply understanding through change. When this transformation is present:

- important context remains recoverable after personnel and system changes;

- decisions remain explainable and reconstructable through their rationale, evidence, authority, assumptions, dependencies, and outcomes;

- knowledge can be reused instead of repeatedly reconstructed or rediscovered;

- accountability remains visible when work crosses teams, suppliers, systems, or AI boundaries;

- changes can be introduced without silently losing intent, continuity, or control; and

- conflicts, missing evidence, outdated assumptions, and material uncertainty become visible early enough to affect action.

This is not a promise that change becomes risk-free or that every past decision can be reconstructed perfectly. It is a commitment to improve the organization’s ability to understand, decide, act, adapt, and remain accountable through change.

Enterprises draw on ShiftBy’s capabilities within their own context, responsibilities, and operating arrangements. Their contribution must be assessed through use and outcomes, rather than adoption of capability names or production of artifacts alone.

## 4. What ShiftBy means by understanding

Information records or communicates facts, events, and artifacts. Understanding is the connected, supported context relevant to a decision, action, change, responsibility, or business outcome that explains their meaning, authority, relationships, implications, and provenance.

Understanding may include:

- why a decision was made;

- what evidence supported it;

- what assumptions existed;

- who had authority and accountability;

- what dependencies were affected;

- what outcomes were expected or observed;

- what has changed since the original context; and

- how the relevant elements are connected, so that the reasoning or explanation can be followed and reconstructed.

Understanding is not required to capture every fact or every relationship. It must be proportionate to the need it serves. Connections must not be treated as meaningful merely because records are similar, retrieved together, or stored in the same system.

Preserved understanding must distinguish authority, status, and uncertainty. Unknown, missing, conflicting, inferred, superseded, unresolved, and low-confidence material must remain visible. It must not be silently converted into fact, approval, rationale, or conclusion.

When uncertainty, missing evidence, conflict, or low confidence is material to consequential use, visibility alone is insufficient. It must produce a proportionate consequence: review, escalation, limitation, withholding, or non-action.

## 5. Enterprise Understanding

**Enterprise Understanding is the organizational ability to preserve, connect, retrieve, apply appropriately, and govern the context required to understand, decide, act, adapt, and remain accountable through change.**

Enterprise Understanding is the broader transformation outcome supported by ShiftBy’s current three-capability model. It is not a fourth capability, product, technology, or claim that every relevant organizational concern has been exhaustively classified.

ShiftBy has developed and practises three complementary capabilities: Organizational Memory, Bounded AI, and Human–AI Accountability. Enterprises can leverage their contributions within their own context; the capabilities are not a model to adopt unchanged.

Execution and adaptation are operating activities and outcomes supported by the three capabilities, not a separate canonical capability.

---

## 6. The three complementary capabilities

The three capabilities contribute to Enterprise Understanding without replacing the organization’s own authority, context, or responsibilities:

### Organizational Memory

**Contribution:** continuity of organizational understanding beyond individual memory.

Organizational Memory preserves relevant knowledge, context, connected relationships, experience, and learning so that understanding remains reusable and recoverable through change.

### Bounded AI

**Contribution:** reduced drift in AI-supported reasoning and execution.

Bounded AI keeps AI tasks, agents, and coordinated workflows aligned with their intended purpose and applicable enterprise context. It establishes boundaries for prepared inputs, reasoning responsibilities, permitted flow, output acceptance, and resource use, supported by validation and observability. The same bounded approach applies to a single AI task and to a multi-stage or multi-agent workflow; it does not require autonomous agents. Its purpose is drift reduction, not a guarantee of error-free reasoning or execution.

### Human–AI Accountability

**Contribution:** sustained human and organizational answerability for AI-enabled work.

Human–AI Accountability sustains human and organizational answerability through explicit ownership, responsibility, and legitimate authority that are exercised and evidenced throughout AI-enabled work. It applies whenever AI contributes to understanding, decisions, recommendations, or consequential execution.

In ShiftBy’s current practice, the capabilities operate together: Organizational Memory sustains continuity of understanding; Bounded AI reduces drift when AI interprets, applies, or acts on context; and Human–AI Accountability applies across both. Their contributions are complementary, not interchangeable.

These responsibilities operate together rather than as a mandatory sequence or maturity ladder. Human–AI Accountability is a cross-cutting condition, not a final approval layer added after memory or AI work is complete.

### Capability outcome test

The contribution of a capability must be supported by evidence appropriate to the organizational context and the intended improvement in the ability to:

- understand relevant context;

- make or review defensible decisions;

- act with appropriate authority;

- adapt while preserving intent and continuity; or

- remain accountable for consequential outcomes.

Appropriate evidence may include reduced reconstruction effort, faster or better-grounded review, reduced dependence on individual memory, reduced drift from intended purpose or permitted flow, earlier identification of missing evidence or dependencies, clearer ownership of consequential actions, safer change adoption, or more consistent handling of uncertainty.

The exact measures depend on the organizational context. Evidence of organizational outcomes is distinct from evidence supporting a Doctrine governance decision. Capability evidence should distinguish: (a) evidence of capability operation in ShiftBy’s practice, (b) evidence that its contributions are used in the relevant enterprise context, and (c) evidence that this use improves business outcomes. Internal practice alone does not establish effectiveness in every enterprise context. Governance evidence supports a decision, revision, or closure process and does not itself establish Doctrine authority. Producing documents, models, workflows, classifications, relationships, or AI outputs is not by itself evidence that a capability or transformation has been achieved.

A capability is justified by the organizational value and risk reduction it enables, not by the sophistication of its records, models, workflows, relationships, or AI mechanisms.

The absence of a recorded relationship, explanation, or evidence must not be interpreted as proof that none existed.

This is ShiftBy’s current three-capability model. It may be revised through a future Founder decision and is not claimed to be universally exhaustive.

## 7. ShiftBy’s role and boundaries

ShiftBy is an executive advisory and consulting practice focused on helping organizations preserve, develop, and apply the understanding required to transform responsibly through change.

ShiftBy may help organizations:

- diagnose gaps in organizational understanding and their business consequences;

- apply relevant contributions from the three complementary capabilities to the organization’s needs and context;

- clarify transformation outcomes, decision conditions, responsibilities, and decision rights;

- establish appropriate evidence, provenance, governance, quality, and assurance expectations;

- assess where bounded and accountable AI participation can improve feasibility, scale, coverage, continuity, timeliness, and usefulness;

- guide adoption of relevant practices and implementation planning within the organization’s own operating arrangements; and

- assess whether the use of those contributions is improving meaningful organizational outcomes.

Workshops, coaching, training, facilitation, implementation guidance, and enablement are delivery modes within this practice. They are not separate ShiftBy capabilities or software products.

ShiftBy may enable client teams to implement and operate capability-related practices, but does not thereby assume responsibility for implementing or operating the client’s systems. ShiftBy may perform bounded assessment, testing, evaluation, or challenge activities where these support its advisory role and the development or evaluation of the three capabilities. Such activity does not make ShiftBy the implementer, systems integrator, or operator of the client’s production systems. Implementation guidance and enablement do not by themselves mean that ShiftBy builds, deploys, integrates, or operates a client’s software, AI models, agents, databases, or production systems.

Client organizations remain responsible for their decisions, systems, data, implementation choices, legal and regulatory obligations, and consequential actions. ShiftBy’s advice, analysis, and AI-assisted outputs remain advisory or provisional unless appropriately reviewed, accepted, and authorized by the responsible organization.

## 8. AI participation in ShiftBy’s practice

AI is integral to ShiftBy’s current practice of developing and applying the three capabilities. This describes ShiftBy’s practice, not a claim that organizational memory in every organization requires AI or autonomous agents.

AI supports interpretation, analysis, retrieval, synthesis, testing, monitoring, gap detection, dependency identification, review, planning, communication, execution support, and governed adjustment within defined boundaries. At enterprise scale, these activities may otherwise become too costly, slow, fragmented, inconsistent, incomplete, or difficult to sustain through human effort alone.

These improvements may reduce manual reconstruction, support faster review, improve continuity during turnover, reduce missed dependencies, surface uncertainty earlier, and help the organization adapt without losing intent.

AI strengthens the feasibility and usefulness of the work, but preserving and applying understanding remains ShiftBy’s purpose.

Preserved understanding may improve the conditions for future model- and agent-supported analysis. It does not guarantee that an AI system will interpret material correctly, preserve all qualifiers, identify every relationship, or produce a safe or valid result.

Canonical capability documents define capability meaning, scope, contribution, and boundaries. Detailed technical mechanisms, control requirements, and operating procedures belong in separate AI specifications, policy, assurance, governance, or implementation documents, not in this Doctrine.

## 9. Conditions for trustworthy AI enablement

These Doctrine-level conditions govern trustworthy AI participation; the safeguards listed below illustrate their application. Their use must be proportionate to the purpose, consequence, affected parties, organizational policy, and applicable law or regulation. They do not constitute a complete or independently enforceable legal, regulatory, cybersecurity, privacy, responsible-AI, model-risk, assurance, technical implementation, or operating control framework.

AI capability does not imply AI authorization. The responsible organization defines authority, responsibility, oversight, review, escalation, intervention, and approval appropriate to the use and its consequences. AI must not independently acquire or assume organizational authority for consequential decisions. Bounded AI execution may operate only within authority, conditions, oversight, and intervention rights established by the responsible organization. Human authority remains responsible for meaning, approval, accountability, risk acceptance, and consequential use. For consequential uses, an appropriately responsible human or organization must review, approve, reject, override, escalate, or otherwise govern AI participation as appropriate to consequence and risk. Non-consequential assistance may receive proportionate oversight without implying that every output requires formal approval.

AI-generated outputs must remain distinguishable from source-grounded knowledge, approved canonical meaning, and human or organizational decisions. The governed process and its records must preserve, where applicable:

- provenance and source references;

- authority, ownership, and decision rights;

- current version and validity period;

- superseded, conflicting, inferred, and uncertain status;

- review and validation status;

- AI participation, system role, and relevant model or process context; and

- applicable human or organizational accountability.

AI-generated recommendations, classifications, relationships, reconstructions, forecasts, instructions, and communication outputs do not acquire authority through generation, repetition, storage, retrieval, or reuse. They require appropriate evidence, validation, approval, or other authorized disposition before they may be treated as accepted organizational understanding or used for consequential purposes.

Understanding used for consequential analysis or action must be appropriately current, versioned, authority-marked, evidence-supported, validated, persisted, and accessible only within applicable permissions and responsibilities.

Trustworthy enablement requires safeguards proportionate to consequence, risk, irreversibility, sensitivity, dependency, and stakeholder impact. Depending on the use, safeguards may include:

- deterministic contracts, schemas, permissions, and state controls;

- oversight, escalation, containment, review, and override;

- protection of sensitive information, access boundaries, provenance, and system integrity;

- appropriate transparency, fairness, contestability, and responsible use;

- validation, quality checks, uncertainty handling, and failure containment;

- observability of relevant AI participation, system behaviour, semantic quality, detected issues, the information and controls used, validations performed, and who approved consequential use;

- persistence and reconstructability of inputs, outputs, evidence, validations, decisions, and approvals; and

- explicit ownership, authority, review, and responsibility.

AI outputs must remain subject to appropriate evidence, validation, provenance, and fail-closed controls. Where required evidence, authority, integrity, validation, or accountability is absent or materially uncertain, the system or process must fail closed through review, escalation, limitation, withholding, or non-action. Uncertainty, missing evidence, contradiction, low confidence, and potential semantic or behavioural failure must not be silently converted into certainty or authority.

These safeguards are conditions of AI enablement, not separate technology features or substitutes for the three capabilities. AI may help make accountability arrangements visible, testable, and enforceable; it does not create or assume organizational accountability.

## 10. Boundaries and exclusions

This Doctrine does not prescribe:

- software architecture or data models;

- particular AI models, agents, databases, or retrieval systems;

- workflows, deployment patterns, or operating procedures;

- detailed control frameworks or compliance procedures; or

- a required implementation sequence or product design.

Technology and implementation mechanisms support the three capabilities. They do not establish Doctrine authority, constitute additional capabilities, or by themselves make ShiftBy a software or AI implementation company.

A database is not Organizational Memory. Stored records or semantic objects constitute Organizational Memory only when they provide relevant, reusable, recoverable, and governed context.

An AI task, agent, or workflow is not by itself Bounded AI. Bounded AI requires defined boundaries, validation, and observability that support reduction of drift from intended purpose and applicable context across reasoning and execution.

An AI system is not Human–AI Accountability. Human and organizational answerability must be sustained throughout its use.

A technical workflow is not Enterprise Understanding. Enterprise Understanding depends on the governed preservation, connection, retrieval, application, and use of context—not on the existence of a particular technology or workflow.

ShiftBy’s advisory role does not transfer client authority, accountability, legal responsibility, or operational ownership to ShiftBy.

## 11. Founder principle and controlled status

Organizations do not lose confidence simply because execution changes.

Organizations lose confidence when the understanding required to explain and govern change is lost.

Preserving understanding allows organizations to adapt without losing confidence. That is the purpose of ShiftBy.
